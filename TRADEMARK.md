# Coaxio trademark policy

The Coaxio **code** is open source. The Coaxio **name and mark** are not, and this document
explains the difference.

Licences in this repository cover copyright: the right to copy, modify and redistribute the files.
They do not grant permission to use "Coaxio" or the Coaxio mark as the identifier of your own
project or product. That is a separate question, and this is the answer to it.

The point is not to restrict what you can build. It is so that someone who installs something
called Coaxio knows where it came from.

## Scope

This policy concerns the word **Coaxio** and the coaxial cross-section mark, as used to identify
open source infrastructure tooling and related software, documentation and websites.

It makes no claim over the word "coax" or "coaxial", over other names built on that root, or over
uses of "Coaxio" outside software infrastructure. Several unrelated parties use similar names in
other fields and this policy does not concern them.

## You may do all of this without asking

- Refer to Coaxio by name when writing or speaking about it: articles, tutorials, reviews,
  comparisons, conference talks, course material, social posts.
- State factual compatibility: "works with Coaxio", "generates Coaxio-compatible configuration",
  "built on Coaxio".
- Cite Coaxio in the documentation, README or dependency list of another project.
- Redistribute unmodified Coaxio releases under their original name.
- Use the mark to link to a Coaxio project, at a size where it is legible and with its clear space
  intact.
- Reproduce the mark in an article, slide or video that discusses Coaxio.

No permission is needed for any of the above, and none will be asked of you.

## Please ask first for these

Anything that could suggest Coaxio is the origin of, or endorses, something it is not:

- Using "Coaxio" in the name of your own project, product, service or company, including as a
  prefix or suffix.
- Registering `coaxio` or a `coaxio-*` name on a package registry (npm, PyPI, crates.io,
  Docker Hub, Homebrew) or as a GitHub organisation.
- Registering a domain containing "coaxio", or a social handle.
- Using the mark as the icon or logo of your own application or service.
- Merchandise.
- Any commercial use of the name or mark beyond the factual references listed above.

## Modified versions

Fork freely — that is what the licence is for. But a modified build must not be distributed under
the Coaxio name or mark.

If you publish a fork, change the name and replace the mark. You are welcome and encouraged to say
what it is derived from: "a fork of Coaxio", "based on coaxio-forge" are both accurate and both
fine. What is not fine is distributing your build *as* Coaxio, because the person installing it has
no way to tell.

This mirrors how Debian and Mozilla handle the same problem, and it exists for the same reason:
the maintainer cannot vouch for code they did not write.

## Attribution

When you mention Coaxio in a way that warrants it, this line is sufficient:

> Coaxio is a trademark of the Coaxio project.

Not required in casual prose or a passing reference.

## Requests and questions

Anything not covered here, or a case where the answer seems to be no but you think it should be
yes: open an issue on this repository, or write to the address in the project README. Permission is
granted freely where there is no risk of confusion about origin, which is most of the time.

If you believe someone is using the name or mark in a way that misleads people about origin, the
same channels apply.

## Status

Coaxio is used as an unregistered mark. Under Italian law an unregistered mark
(*marchio di fatto*, art. 2571 civil code and art. 12 of the Industrial Property Code) acquires
protection through actual and continuous use in commerce, limited to the territory and the goods
or services in which that use can be demonstrated.

Registration has not been filed. This document records the intended scope of the mark and, with
the project's commit history, its use over time. Nothing here is legal advice.
